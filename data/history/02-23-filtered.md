# On This Day: Mon Feb 23 — Curated

### Events

1. **532** — Byzantine emperor Justinian I lays the foundation stone of the Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, one of the great architectural achievements of human civilization.

2. **1455** — Traditional date of publication of the Gutenberg Bible, the first Western book printed with movable type, transforming the spread of knowledge.

3. **1725** — J.S. Bach premieres his Shepherd Cantata for the birthday of Christian, Duke of Saxe-Weissenfels.

4. **1886** — Charles Martin Hall discovers an inexpensive electrolytic method of producing aluminium, making the metal accessible and launching the aluminium age.

5. **1898** — Émile Zola is imprisoned in France after writing *J'Accuse…!*, his open letter defending Alfred Dreyfus against antisemitism and a judicial conspiracy — a landmark in press freedom and civil courage.

6. **1909** — The AEA Silver Dart makes the first powered heavier-than-air flight in Canada and the British Empire.

7. **1917** — First demonstrations in Saint Petersburg begin the February Revolution, setting in motion the end of 300 years of Romanov rule and reshaping the world order.

8. **1927** — Werner Heisenberg writes to Wolfgang Pauli describing his uncertainty principle for the first time, a cornerstone of quantum mechanics.

9. **1941** — Plutonium is first chemically identified by Glenn T. Seaborg and his team at the University of California, Berkeley.

10. **1954** — The first mass inoculation of children against polio with the Salk vaccine begins in Pittsburgh, a milestone in public health.

11. **1987** — SN 1987A, the closest supernova in centuries, is observed in the Large Magellanic Cloud — the first supernova modern astronomers could study in detail.

---

### Births

12. **1633** — Samuel Pepys, English diarist and naval administrator, whose diaries are an irreplaceable primary source for 17th-century English life (died 1703).

13. **1685** — George Frideric Handel, German-English composer of the *Messiah*, *Water Music*, and *Music for the Royal Fireworks* (died 1759).

14. **1744** — Mayer Amschel Rothschild, founder of the Rothschild banking dynasty (died 1812).

15. **1868** — W.E.B. Du Bois, American sociologist, historian, and civil rights activist; co-founder of the NAACP (died 1963).

16. **1878** — Kazimir Malevich, Ukrainian-Russian painter who pioneered Suprematism and abstract art (died 1935).

17. **1884** — Casimir Funk, Polish biochemist who coined the term "vitamine" and demonstrated that deficiency diseases such as beriberi and scurvy had dietary causes (died 1967).

18. **1889** — Victor Fleming, American director who helmed both *Gone with the Wind* and *The Wizard of Oz* in the same year (1939) (died 1949).

19. **1949** — Marc Garneau, Canadian engineer and the first Canadian in space (died 2025).

20. **1960** — Naruhito, Emperor of Japan.

21. **1965** — Michael Dell, American entrepreneur and founder of Dell Technologies.

22. **1975** — Robert Lopez, American songwriter and playwright; EGOT winner, composer of *Avenue Q*, *The Book of Mormon*, and "Let It Go" from *Frozen*.

23. **1983** — Emily Blunt, English actress.

---

### Deaths

24. **1821** — John Keats, English Romantic poet (*Ode to a Nightingale*, *Ode on a Grecian Urn*) (born 1795).

25. **1855** — Carl Friedrich Gauss, German mathematician, astronomer, and physicist; one of the most influential mathematicians in history (born 1777).

26. **1931** — Nellie Melba, Australian soprano of international renown; the first Australian to achieve global operatic fame (born 1861).

27. **1934** — Edward Elgar, English composer (*Enigma Variations*, *Pomp and Circumstance*) (born 1857).

28. **1944** — Leo Baekeland, Belgian-American chemist who invented Bakelite, the world's first fully synthetic plastic, pioneering the plastics age (born 1863).

29. **1965** — Stan Laurel, English comedian of the legendary Laurel and Hardy duo (born 1890).

30. **1995** — James Herriot, English veterinarian and author of *All Creatures Great and Small* (born 1916).
